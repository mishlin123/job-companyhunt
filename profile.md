## Work rights

- Dual **New Zealand and British** citizen (NZ and UK passports). Age 22 (23 from December 2026), which matters for working-holiday and youth schemes.
- **NZ**: full work rights.
- **Australia**: NZ citizens can live and work there without sponsorship (Special Category Visa, granted on arrival). Roles open to "Australian or New Zealand citizens" are fine. Roles requiring Australian citizenship or permanent residency only (some government, defence and security-cleared posts) are a blocker.
- **UK**: no sponsorship needed.
- **Ireland**: straightforward under the Common Travel Area.
- **EU** (Belgium, Netherlands, Germany, Denmark, Sweden, Finland, France, Austria, Spain, Bulgaria): needs a work permit. Note whether a posting offers relocation or visa support. Programmes that require permanent EU/Swiss work rights (e.g. J&J GOLD) are a hurdle.
- **Switzerland**: not in the EU; needs a Swiss work permit. Note whether a posting offers relocation or permit support.
- **US**: sponsorship is the major constraint. Note whether a posting mentions it.
- **Canada, Singapore, Israel**: note any citizenship, residency or visa requirement a posting states.
- **Routes that don't need an employer sponsor** (checked Oct 2026; I'm within every age limit below):
  - **Germany**: the Canterbury MSc counts as equal to a German Master's (anabin H+), so any matching job offer qualifies for a skilled-worker permit with no salary floor. NZ and UK citizens can enter visa-free and apply from inside Germany. The NZ working-holiday visa (18–30) also allows up to a year with one employer.
  - **Switzerland**: the NZ passport qualifies for the Young Professionals scheme (18–30, up to 18 months, in my field of training, no labour-market test). UK citizens have their own permit quota, but employers must still prefer Swiss/EU candidates.
  - **Canada**: International Experience Canada on the UK passport (18–35; up to 24 months, then 12 more) gives an open work permit. Apply when the 2027 pool opens (around Dec 2026 or Jan 2027).
  - **Singapore**: the Work Holiday Programme (graduates aged 18–25, apply before turning 26; 6 months), then an Employment Pass. "Novel food biotechnologist" is on the Shortage Occupation List.
  - **US**: a J-1 internship (within 12 months of graduating, up to 12 months) or a cap-exempt employer (universities, national labs). The H-1B lottery is a poor bet for a new graduate.
- **Routes that need the employer's help**: the **Netherlands** (an IND recognised sponsor and at least €4,357 a month under 30), **Denmark** (Pay Limit scheme or a SIRI Fast-track certified employer), **Sweden** (90% of median salary and 10 days' advertising), **Belgium, France and Austria** (national highly-qualified permits). NZ working-holiday visas for these countries cap work at 3–6 months per employer, so they are only a bridge.
- **Sponsorship evidence**: everywhere except NZ, Australia, the UK and Ireland, note in a role's notes whether the employer sponsors visas (the posting says so, or the watchlist row records it, e.g. a recognised-sponsor register or recent H-1B filings). A posting that says it offers no sponsorship is a blocker. Rate such roles `signal` at most.

## How to judge fit

- **strong**: entry-level or MSc-level role in interests 1–4, or a relevant graduate programme; start timing can work for July 2027; no obvious work-rights blocker.
- **possible**: relevant, but with a real caveat. For example: asks for 2–4 years' experience, an adjacent field (cell therapy, analytical chemistry, QC), tight timing, or unclear sponsorship.
- **signal**: not applicable as it stands (senior, PhD required, 5+ years, must start now), but shows the company is hiring in a relevant team. Useful for a speculative application.
- **Leave out**: summer internships and other roles that need work before July 2027 (but note a recurring internship or graduate intake as a lead for the next cycle); roles outside science and process (sales, finance, HR, IT, marketing, legal, admin, senior leadership), and unrelated sites or functions at the big multinationals.
